Video games are increasingly used to complement treatments for anxiety and depression, but each game is described
on its own terms, which makes it hard to compare them or to learn from one to the next. My PhD thesis, *Video
game-based interventions for anxiety and depression: from design to effectiveness*, addresses this gap.

- **Systematic review:** 39 randomised trials covering 25 games, each coded against 47 design principles and four
  types of player engagement. Clustering revealed four design profiles that cut across therapeutic approaches.
- **Meta-analysis:** 31 effect sizes from 19 trials show that game-based interventions produce a small but reliable
  reduction in anxiety and depression symptoms (Hedges' *g* = 0.25).
- **Reporting blueprint:** a proposal to document the design of therapeutic games so that design choices become
  transparent, searchable and shareable.

The data extraction for both syntheses relied on [llmde](../llmde/), and the review's design taxonomy was applied
to [Eco-Rescue](../eco-rescue/).
